# Define the owner approval protocol

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Assigned: dev
Parent: [Design and launch reusable Restricted Group Policy](../map.md)
Blocked by: 02

## Question

What durable owner-only state machine coordinates decisions across the exact owner Control Channel and local TUI for persistent Capability Grants, current-task Temporary Elevations, inferred capability proposals, PII/NDA egress releases, and Shared Knowledge Candidate promotions? Accept the first authenticated owner decision exactly once, apply the correct exact-group/function/resource/fingerprint/lifetime scope, re-check authority, resume or terminate the suspended operation, and mark duplicate surfaces resolved. Decide interruption, restart, stale or duplicate responses, revocation, audit, notification privacy, and prompt-cache-stable behavior without treating non-owner Control Channel operational authority as owner governance.

## Comments

- Grilling Q1: the exact owner-designated Control Channel and the local TUI are both authoritative `Approval Surfaces`. They receive the same approval request; the first valid authenticated-owner terminal decision wins and the other surface becomes resolved. Owner messages in other conversations retain full operational authority but cannot submit Approval Request or revocation decisions.
- Grilling Q2: both Approval Surfaces may display sensitive approval content. Designating a Control Channel explicitly authorizes that exact management conversation to receive the decision payload even when it contains PII, NDA, or owner-private data; plaintext credentials, tokens, cookies, and auth secrets remain a non-overridable hard deny.
- Grilling Q3: each approval type displays only its exact decision payload, not unrelated upstream context. A PII/NDA release shows the exact proposed reply or artifact preview; knowledge promotion shows the exact candidate and provenance; Temporary Elevation shows the proposed source and scope without pre-reading it; Capability Grant and inferred classification show descriptor, effects, resource scope, and implementation fingerprint. Unrelated prompts, conversations, records, and attachments are omitted.
- Grilling Q4: the originating Restricted Group receives only a short request ID, approval type, sanitized capability summary, `waiting_for_owner` state, `/stop` availability, and the eventual `approved/resumed`, `denied`, `expired`, or `cancelled` outcome. It never receives the Approval Surface identity, TUI state, sensitive decision payload, owner presence, or decision rationale.
- Grilling Q5: an owner may `Approve exact`, `Deny`, or `Edit proposal`. Edit never grants authority: it marks the original request `superseded`, creates a new request ID with the complete revised scope/lifetime, and requires a fresh `Approve exact`, ensuring the persisted decision exactly matches the proposal the owner confirmed.
- Grilling Q6-Q7: each Approval Request remains first-wins, immutable, and exactly once; an identical retry is idempotent. The owner's latest intent controls current authority without rewriting history: a later opposite intent must create and confirm a new owner governance request against the current effective scope, then revoke or grant authority at the next executable boundary. A resolved card is only a history/status projection and may open that new request, but cannot silently mutate authority; already-started operations retain the original decision and revision evidence.
- Grilling Q8-Q9: `Deny` always asks the owner to choose `Deny once` or `Deny always`. Once terminates only the exact request. Always cannot infer a fuzzy similar-request class: it creates a new proposal containing the smallest reusable exact group/function/resource-or-provenance/destination/fingerprint/type selector, permits owner editing, and requires a second `Confirm persistent denial`; if no explicit selector can be formed, only Deny once is available.
- Grilling Q10: approval lifetimes are fixed by type. Capability Grants and approved inferred classifications persist until owner revocation but invalidate on implementation-fingerprint change; Temporary Elevations last only for the exact durable task and expire when that task completes, aborts, or merges, or when the owner revokes them; a PII/NDA release authorizes one exact-payload/exact-destination logical delivery with idempotent transport retry; Shared Knowledge promotion authorizes one exact-candidate/provenance/destination idempotent promotion; a Persistent Denial lasts until owner revocation.
- Grilling Q11: a task waiting for owner approval becomes suspended and releases the group's active execution slot, so later queued tasks may continue. The group still executes at most one runnable task at a time; suspension is not parallel execution, and the approval-bound task preserves its own exact task identity, isolated state, and Approval Request rather than merging with later group work.
- Grilling Q12: approval never preempts the group's currently running task. An approved suspended task enters the front of the resume-ready queue and runs next after the current task; multiple resumed tasks keep original pause order. Before resuming, Hermes revalidates the current policy/grant/elevation, implementation fingerprint, task state, and required artifacts. Denied, expired, cancelled, or invalid tasks terminate instead of entering the resume queue.
- Grilling Q13: an operation-bound pending Approval Request expires after 24 hours. Expiry is not denial and creates no denial rule; Hermes removes its sensitive decision payload, suspended checkpoint, and temporary artifacts, sends only the safe `expired` status to the origin group, and requires a new task/request if the work is still needed. The owner policy may configure the deadline, group members cannot extend it, and Shared Knowledge Candidate retention is decided separately.
- Grilling Q14-Q16: all unexpired pending requests, metadata-only task checkpoint state, and Temporary Elevations survive process restart and are reconstructed automatically; the 24-hour deadline continues across downtime. Process lifetime is not task lifetime. A durable task becomes terminal only when completed, aborted, or explicitly merged into another task; merge never transfers its elevation, and owner revocation still removes authority. Resume revalidates current policy and operation completion markers so already-completed side effects are never replayed.
- Grilling Q17: any member of the exact origin group may abort one of that group's suspended tasks with `/stop <short-request-id>`, and the owner may cancel it from either Approval Surface. Bare `/stop` affects only the current active task. Cross-group IDs are rejected, terminal IDs only return status, and abort removes checkpoint, sensitive payload, sandbox, and Temporary Elevation.
- Grilling Q18: task completion, abort, or merge atomically cancels every still-pending Approval Request originating from that task; late decisions are terminal/stale and cannot create authority. Persistent grants or denials that already took effect remain until separate owner revocation, while Temporary Elevation ends with the task. A task-terminal versus owner-decision race is resolved by durable compare-and-set and both attempted outcomes are audited.
- Grilling Q19: durable approval/task state stores metadata only: stable identities, source locator/version, content and payload fingerprints, policy/descriptor revisions, state/deadline, and a completed-operation journal. It never persists raw sensitive decision payloads or checkpoints. After restart Hermes reacquires content from the original source under current authority; if the source is unavailable or no longer authorized, the task blocks or expires rather than reconstructing content from metadata.
- Grilling Q20: reacquired source and exact decision payload must match the recorded fingerprint, scope, destination, and provenance before a pending or approved request can be reused. Any change marks the old request `superseded_stale` and creates a new request for owner confirmation. The completed-operation journal prevents replay of already-finished side effects.
- Grilling Q21: persistent Capability Grants, Persistent Denials, and revocations require a short owner rationale; Temporary Elevations, one-time PII/NDA releases, Shared Knowledge promotions, and Deny once allow an optional rationale. Rationale is visible only on Approval Surfaces and in the content-minimized audit, never returned to the Restricted Group. Plaintext credentials/auth secrets are rejected, and sensitive business rationale is retained only by fingerprint rather than raw text.
- Grilling Q22: Hermes durably creates the canonical request and suspends the task before projecting it to both Approval Surfaces through a retryable outbox. Either reachable surface may decide without waiting for the other; delivery failure never fails open or enables another channel. If neither surface is reachable the request remains pending until its 24-hour deadline, and a recovered surface displays a terminal result rather than reopening a resolved request.
- Grilling Q23: owner revocation changes effective authority at the next executable boundary without automatically aborting affected tasks. Already-started operations retain their recorded start revision and egress is rechecked; later operations requiring revoked authority are blocked. The same task must not automatically open an identical approval request after revocation, though it may finish along an authorized path. Fresh group work or a new owner governance action is required to request that authority again.
- Grilling Q24: selecting Deny always first atomically applies Deny once to the current Approval Request, immediately terminating that blocked operation, and separately creates the scoped Persistent Denial proposal. Only the second confirmation affects future requests. If that proposal is abandoned or expires, the current request stays denied once and no persistent rule exists.
- Grilling Q25: changing the owner-designated Control Channel invalidates every pending action projection in the old channel. Canonical requests remain pending and are reprojected through the outbox to the new exact channel while local TUI remains authoritative. Old cards return `surface_reconfigured`; the reconfiguration and affected request IDs are audited because already-displayed payload cannot be recalled. Requests never move across profiles.
- Grilling Q26: a Persistent Denial, revoke, or owner-initiated persistent-policy edit proposal created as a standalone governance action is detached from the originating task, so that task becoming terminal does not cancel it. Each standalone governance proposal expires after 24 hours; expiry does not change current authority and a later change requires a new proposal against current exact scope. Editing a task-bound pending proposal under Q5 remains task-bound. Shared Knowledge Candidate retention remains ticket 13.

## Answer

Use one active-profile-local `ApprovalCoordinator` with a canonical durable `ApprovalRequest` record, transactional policy mutation, a retryable projection outbox, isolated durable task metadata, and an append-only event stream. Teams cards, TUI views, and the origin-group status are projections; none of them is authority or a second request.

### Canonical identity and authentication

Each request records an opaque globally unique ID plus short display ID; active profile; request kind; exact tenant/conversation, requester-principal, policy, policy revision, durable-task, parent-operation, function, normalized resource, effect, implementation, source/provenance, destination, and payload fingerprints; proposed lifetime; creation/deadline; current state/version; and completed-operation/idempotency markers. Raw sensitive prompts, source content, decision payloads, attachments, credentials, and auth material are not persisted.

Control Channel decisions are accepted only when both the immutable authenticated sender principal and exact tenant/conversation/config revision match the active profile's configured owner and current Control Channel. Local TUI decisions require the same active profile and a trusted local owner session. Display names, group membership, non-owner Control Channel operational authority, model claims, and messages from any other conversation never establish governance authority.

Both Approval Surfaces may display the type-specific exact decision payload, including PII, NDA, or owner-private content, because the owner explicitly designated them for management. They never display plaintext credentials, tokens, cookies, or auth secrets. The origin Restricted Group sees only the short ID, kind, sanitized summary, waiting state, cancellation affordance, and terminal outcome.

### State machine and exactly-once decision

1. At an approval boundary, Hermes validates the Capability Request, creates the canonical request and `pending` event durably, records its expected policy/descriptor/source/payload revisions, suspends the task, strips raw sensitive local state, and only then emits outbox projections. No notification is sent before durable creation.
2. `Approve exact`, `Deny`, or `Edit proposal` is checked against owner identity, active profile, current Approval Surface revision, request version/state/deadline, policy revision, task state, implementation fingerprint, and exact proposal fingerprints.
3. The first valid terminal transition from `pending` wins through compare-and-set. Applying a grant, elevation, one-time release, promotion authority, or denial and recording the winning Owner Decision occur atomically. An exact retry returns the existing result idempotently; a conflicting late response returns `already_resolved` and cannot rewrite history.
4. `Edit proposal` transitions the original to `superseded`, creates a new request ID with the complete changed proposal, and requires fresh `Approve exact`. Refetched content or metadata that no longer matches transitions to `superseded_stale` and follows the same new-request path.
5. `Deny` asks `Deny once` or `Deny always`. Once terminates only that request. Always first atomically applies Deny once to the current request, immediately terminating that blocked operation, then creates a separate task-detached, smallest-explicit-scope Persistent Denial proposal. Owner edits, a required rationale, and a second confirmation are required; fuzzy similarity is forbidden.
6. `expired`, `cancelled`, `superseded`, `superseded_stale`, `approved`, and `denied_once` are terminal for the original request. `surface_reconfigured` and `already_resolved` are projection/response statuses, not canonical states.
7. A later opposite owner intent creates a new governance request against current effective authority. The latest confirmed intent changes current authority, but every prior Owner Decision and already-started side effect remains immutable.

### Type-specific payload and lifetime

- A Capability Grant or approved Inferred Capability Proposal shows descriptor, effects, exact function/resource/group, and implementation fingerprint. It persists until owner revocation and invalidates on implementation-fingerprint change.
- A Temporary Elevation shows the private source and exact read scope without pre-reading it. It binds one exact Durable Task, survives process restart, and expires only when the task completes, aborts, or merges, or when the owner revokes it. Merge never transfers it.
- A PII/NDA release shows the exact proposed reply/artifact and authorizes one exact-payload/exact-destination logical delivery. Retries must reuse the same idempotency identity.
- A Shared Knowledge promotion shows the exact candidate, provenance, sensitivity, and destination and authorizes one idempotent promotion. Candidate retention remains owned by ticket 13.
- A Persistent Denial shows one explicit reusable selector and persists until owner revocation.
- An Owner Risk Exception shows one fully evidenced known failure, concrete consequences, and the exact profile, production group, policy/build version, function, and owner-selected applied lifetime. Each failure has a separate request, never covers missing evidence, and remains revocable until its applied lifetime ends or the exact version retires. Its still-pending Approval Request separately expires after 24 hours.
- A Production Activation Proposal shows its exact identity envelope, Activation Evidence Manifest, verified rollback target or exact accepted rollback-readiness failure, progressive activation plan, and every accepted Owner Risk Exception ID. Its still-pending request expires after 24 hours and authorizes one idempotent activation journal only after a separate final `Approve exact`.
- A post-opening resume decision shows the held operation or blocked exact function, production evidence, LLM inference and confidence when present, current policy/build identity, and known Owner Risk Exception if required. It never applies to another operation, function, version, group, or profile.

Persistent grants, denials, and revocations require a short owner rationale. Task-bound and one-time decisions allow an optional rationale. Rationale never goes to the Restricted Group; plaintext secrets are rejected, and sensitive rationale is retained only by fingerprint.

### Suspension, queue, resume, and termination

A waiting task becomes suspended and releases the group's runnable slot, so later queued tasks continue while the group still runs at most one runnable task at a time. Each suspended task preserves an isolated context identity; later group work is not merged into it implicitly.

Approval never preempts a running task. Once approved, the suspended task enters the front of the resume-ready queue and runs after the current task; multiple resumed tasks keep original pause order. Resume revalidates current authority, policy and descriptor revisions, task status, source/payload fingerprints, artifacts, and operation journal before any executable boundary.

Any member of the exact origin group may `/stop <short-id>` its suspended task; the owner may cancel it from either Approval Surface. Bare `/stop` still affects only the current active task. Completion, abort (including `/stop` or unrecoverable failure), or explicit merge atomically cancels every still-pending request from that task, deletes task-local state, and ends its Temporary Elevation. Late decisions cannot create authority. Persistent authority already applied remains until separately revoked.

Operation-bound pending requests expire after 24 hours by default; the owner policy may configure this deadline and group members may not extend it. Expiry is not denial. It removes task metadata and temporary state, emits a safe origin-group status, and requires a new request if work is still needed.

Standalone Persistent Denial, revoke, and owner-initiated persistent-policy edit governance proposals also expire after 24 hours but do not end merely because the source task becomes terminal. A Q5 edit of a task-bound pending proposal remains task-bound. Expiry leaves current authority unchanged. Shared Knowledge Candidate retention remains ticket 13.

### Restart and sensitive-state contract

Unexpired canonical requests, outbox state, durable task metadata, Temporary Elevations, deadlines, and completed-operation journals survive restart. Process lifetime does not define task lifetime. Raw sensitive content and raw model/tool checkpoints do not survive: Hermes reacquires source content through the original Capability Provider under current authority, using the recorded source identity/version and provenance.

The reacquired source and regenerated exact decision payload must match every recorded fingerprint before old pending or approved authority is reused. Any content, scope, provenance, destination, policy, or implementation change creates a new request. Missing or unauthorized source data blocks/expires the task. Completed-operation and delivery journals, provider idempotency, and side-effect read-back prevent restart from replaying a completed effect; an indeterminate non-idempotent outcome fails closed for reconciliation rather than retrying blindly.

### Revocation, delivery, profile, audit, and prompt cache

Revocation uses a new exact owner governance request and required rationale. It takes effect at the next executable boundary without automatically aborting the task. Started operations retain their start revision and egress is independently rechecked; later operations requiring revoked authority are blocked, and that same task does not automatically reprompt for the revoked authority.

The outbox independently retries Control Channel and TUI projections. Either reachable surface may decide; one failed surface never fails open or authorizes another channel. If neither is reachable the request remains pending until expiry. A Control Channel reconfiguration invalidates old cards, audits affected IDs, and reprojects pending requests to the new exact channel; already-displayed payload in the old channel cannot be recalled, and no request crosses profiles.

Approval events are append-only and content-minimized: request/decision/parent-operation/task IDs and fingerprints, owner and origin fingerprints, request kind/scope/lifetime fingerprints, policy/descriptor/source revisions, state transitions and compare-and-set version, surface/config revision and delivery state, deadlines, rationale or rationale fingerprint, operation/idempotency markers, revocation, resume, egress, cleanup, restart, reconfiguration, and race outcomes. Raw protected content is excluded and the full retention/tamper/query contract remains ticket 11.

Runtime authority lives outside the model transcript and is re-read at every executable boundary. Approval completes the existing policy-gate continuation; it does not inject a synthetic user message, mutate prior history, rebuild the system prompt, or change tool schemas. New tool schemas still wait for a new session. This preserves strict role alternation and a byte-stable prompt-cache prefix.

### Required proof before implementation is accepted

Ticket 05 must exercise both surface projections, exact payload presentation, edit/supersede, deny once/always, rationale, revoke, reconfiguration, production activation, per-failure Owner Risk Exceptions, direct resume, and origin-group privacy. New ticket 14 must prove transactional first-wins decisions, outbox recovery, concurrent later tasks, 24-hour expiry, abort/merge races, process restart at every transition, metadata-only source reacquisition, fingerprint invalidation, and no side-effect replay. Tickets 09, 11, 12, and 13 own egress, audit, inferred-classification, and knowledge-retention proofs without weakening this protocol.
