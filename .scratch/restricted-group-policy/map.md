# Design and launch reusable Restricted Group Policy

Labels: wayfinder:map

## Destination

Build, deploy, and live-verify a reusable Teams MTK Restricted Group Policy framework whose base member-request authorization is fail-closed and whose authenticated owner may separately accept an exact, fully evidenced implementation-defect risk for production. Its first production policy lets every member of a designated Monitor Group query and export ALPS/MOLY through the owner's account while enforcing global read-only behavior, private-source isolation, per-group temporary storage, origin-bound Teams egress, MTK-internal model routing, privacy-preserving optimization capture, and owner-approved promotion of shared knowledge into the owner's PKB.

The map includes implementation, adversarial E2E, TestGroup rollout, explicit owner production activation, immediate post-opening production probes, rollback readiness, and post-deploy verification; it does not stop at a specification.

## Notes

- Use `grilling`, `domain-modeling`, `research`, `prototype`, `tdd`, `implement`, and the Teams MTK E2E skills where their ticket type requires them.
- Existing spec ownership is preserved rather than duplicated: [`teams-mtk-group-whitelist`](../../openspec/changes/teams-mtk-group-whitelist/) is the completed admission/mention/config/toolset/keyword baseline; [`teams-mtk-hermes-native-parity`](../../openspec/changes/teams-mtk-hermes-native-parity/) owns Teams transport, streaming/reply/file/forward behavior and named real E2E; [`Executable Skill Strategies`](../executable-skill-strategies/spec.md) owns the generic Execution Authority, Capability Provider, Core guardrail, descendant-authority, and fail-closed vocabulary that Restricted Group Policy extends.
- Do not append unresolved Restricted Group Policy requirements to either existing Teams change. Once the Wayfinder decision/prototype frontier and remaining fog are clear, run `/to-spec` to synthesize a new cross-cutting `restricted-group-policy` OpenSpec change that cites those baselines, then `/to-tickets` before implementation. The new spec must preserve group-whitelist admission while replacing toolset/keyword-only restriction as the security model with per-operation capability authority.
- The framework supports multiple Restricted Groups with independent effective permissions. The owner binds each exact tenant/conversation fingerprint to its own policy/version, Capability Grants, Temporary Elevations, sandbox, history scope, and audit stream; approval or modification for one group never changes another group, even when both began from the same template. If identity is ambiguous, stale, or mismatched, fail closed.
- An authenticated owner request retains full owner Execution Authority in every conversation. An owner-designated exact Control Channel is outside Restricted Group Policy: its non-owner members receive full operational authority but never owner identity or owner-only governance. Ordinary owner-private PII/NDA content pauses before multi-member egress and requires explicit owner release; the exact Control Channel and local TUI may display the type-specific sensitive decision payload of a pending approval request, without unrelated context. Plaintext credential/auth output is never allowed in any multi-member Teams conversation, although providers may use credentials internally.
- General group requests require `@hermes`; known control commands such as `/stop` bypass the mention gate.
- Every group member is authorized to query ALPS/MOLY, list/download attachments, and request export. CQ comment, update, assign, and every other CQ mutation are forbidden.
- The policy is globally read-only across external systems. The only write exceptions are replies to the exact origin group, explicitly requested artifact upload to that group, CQ export generation, audit records, and computation inside the group's temporary area.
- Deny all owner-private sources by default: other Teams conversations and DMs, email, local/network files, Workflow, Webex, calendar/meetings, OneNote, Hermes memory/Mem0/PKB/session history/skills/config/logs/credentials, and browser-cookie or owner-session bypasses. ALPS/MOLY is the persistent account-backed read exception. From an Approval Surface, the owner may grant one requesting group a Temporary Elevation for an explicit private-read function and resource scope; it never exposes credentials or secrets, does not rewrite the baseline policy, and retains provenance/origin-egress/audit checks. It belongs to one exact durable task, survives process restart, and expires when that task completes, aborts, or merges, or on owner revocation; it never transfers to the merged target, queued, or subsequent tasks.
- Allow other classified read capabilities unless denied. An unknown or unclassified capability pauses the task and asks the owner through both the exact owner control conversation and local TUI. Owner approval binds one explicit function and normalized resource scope to the requesting group's exact policy, persists until revoked, and resumes the paused task; it never approves a whole plugin, another group, another profile, or global Hermes.
- If a tool, plugin, or MCP operation lacks a valid Capability Descriptor, Hermes may infer a classification proposal from its schema, description, manifest/source evidence, and argument shape. Inference is never authority: the task remains paused until the owner approves the exact function and resource scope. The approval binds the requesting group and implementation/version fingerprint, and any fingerprint change invalidates it for reclassification.
- Owner governance uses one active-profile-local canonical Approval Request projected through a retryable outbox to both authoritative Approval Surfaces: the exact Control Channel and local TUI. The first valid authenticated-owner terminal decision wins immutably (`first-wins` resolution); retries are idempotent, edits and later opposite intent create new requests, and non-owner Control Channel operational authority never becomes governance authority. `Deny once` ends only the exact request; `Deny always` first atomically denies the current request, then opens a detached smallest-explicit-scope Persistent Denial proposal requiring owner rationale, editing, and second confirmation.
- Approval Surfaces may display only the type-specific exact sensitive decision payload, never unrelated context or plaintext credentials/auth secrets. The origin group receives only a short ID, sanitized summary, waiting/cancellation state, and terminal outcome. Operation-bound requests and standalone denial/revoke/persistent-policy-edit governance proposals expire after 24 hours unless owner policy changes the deadline; Shared Knowledge Candidate retention is separate.
- A waiting task suspends and releases the group's active execution slot so later queued tasks continue; suspension is not parallel execution. Approval does not preempt the current task; an approved task resumes next after current work, after fresh authority and fingerprint checks. Same-group members may `/stop <short-id>` suspended work, and task completion, abort, or merge cancels its pending requests and ends its elevation.
- Requests, metadata-only task state, deadlines, outbox, operation journal, and Temporary Elevations survive process restart. Sensitive content/checkpoints do not persist: Hermes re-fetches from the original authorized source, reuses approval only on exact fingerprint match, and creates a new request on any change. Completed or indeterminate side effects are never blindly replayed.
- Policy approvals and revocations take effect at the next executable boundary. An operation already started may finish without cancellation. Its reply/upload is still checked separately: PII, NDA, owner-private, secret, policy-control, brokered-confidential, and unknown data stop after revocation, while non-sensitive output from that already-authorized operation may make a one-time return to the exact origin group. Every later operation uses the new authority, and audit records both start and egress revisions plus actual delivery.
- Only the owner may create, bind, modify, approve, revoke, deploy, or disable a policy. There is no per-user privilege expansion inside a restricted group.
- All main-model, fallback, auxiliary, vision, OCR, embedding, title-generation, and delegated-agent routes must use approved MTK internal AIDE routes; unavailable routes fail closed.
- Each group has an isolated temporary root. Its whole root is cleaned daily at 03:00. If a task is active then, that group's sweep is skipped and the active task directory is deleted immediately when the task ends. `/stop` also deletes the current task directory.
- Each group runs one runnable task at a time. Additional messages use the existing queue, but approval-waiting tasks suspend and release the slot. Any group member may `/stop` the current task or `/stop <short-id>` a suspended task from that exact group; queued work continues afterward. An approved suspended task does not preempt current work and resumes next.
- Same-group follow-up context has no time limit, but comes only from semantically relevant original Teams history in that exact group. Do not create Hermes memory, Mem0, PKB, or cross-group history for it.
- Restricted-group use may automatically emit de-identified Optimization Records containing capability, outcome, failure, correction, and feedback evidence, but no raw prompts, Teams/CQ content, PII, NDA text, or attachments. Content-bearing Shared Knowledge Candidates retain their provenance and enter the owner's PKB only after explicit owner approval; group turns never receive skill-write, provider-source-write, PKB-write, or declassification authority.
- Artifacts successfully uploaded to the origin group are no longer access-restricted by Hermes. Local staging copies remain subject to cleanup.
- There is no business quota for time, rows, attachment size, export size, or sandbox storage.
- Audit is mandatory and content-minimized: record immutable/fingerprinted principal and origin, policy/version, operation, CQ target/query fingerprint, decision/reason, data volume, provider/model, cleanup verdict, and origin-egress verdict; never record credentials, full prompts, full CQ content, attachments, or generated code.
- Rollout is TestGroup adversarial E2E first, then an exact owner Production Activation Proposal. Missing, skipped, zero-case, or unreadable evidence blocks activation; each fully evidenced known defect requires a separate owner exception before final activation approval. There is no locked production canary: the policy opens directly to members, capability slices become available progressively while each operation remains pinned to one policy version, and harmless allow/deny probes run immediately after opening with API, audit, cleanup, and cross-group read-back.
- A post-opening failure never auto-rolls back the whole binding. The demonstrated failed function is blocked while other functions continue. When scope is uncertain, an approved AIDE-routed LLM's self-reported confidence strictly above 90 percent blocks its inferred function; lower confidence holds only the triggering operation for up to 24 hours while the owner decides. The authenticated owner may resume an exact function despite a known defect through an exact, audited, expiring Owner Risk Exception; this accepts implementation risk but does not grant members the prohibited capability.
- The normal activation baseline requires a verified last-known-good rollback target, or a verified disabled state for first activation. Unknown or untested rollback state blocks activation; a fully evidenced known rollback-readiness failure may proceed only through its own exact Owner Risk Exception and must disclose the remaining disable/compensation path. Crash recovery resumes only the unchanged, idempotent activation journal after current identity, AIDE, configuration, owner authority, and rollback checks. Owner rollback moves new work to the prior version or disabled state when available and drains in-flight work only to the next safe executable boundary while blocking unexecuted mutation, egress, and sensitive output.
- Do not commit, push, or deploy merely by working a planning/decision ticket. Execution tickets must preserve unrelated dirty work in the checkout.

## Out of scope

- Granting Monitor Group members any CQ mutation or any external-system write beyond the explicit origin reply/artifact and ephemeral-compute exceptions.
- Exposing owner credentials, cookies, tokens, login secrets, credential paths, or raw authentication failures to the model, sandbox, audit, or group.
- Changing the authenticated owner's full authority in local CLI/TUI or any conversation. Delegated operational authority and mandatory output guards for owner-designated Control Channels are in scope, but never confer owner identity or owner-only governance.
- Automatically ingesting Monitor Group content into the owner's PKB, Mem0, or other private knowledge stores.
