# Key Event #7 — eSIM Extra Bytes / eSIM Mute

## Recommended event title

**eSIM Extra Bytes / eSIM Temporary Mute Debugging**

## What was discussed and what was decided

- The eSIM temporary-mute symptom appeared in multiple dog-folder issue reports and was difficult to converge because the initial reports did not consistently provide a structured debugging trace covering the full transaction.
- The P26 eSIM-only SKU also limited direct observability. The signal had to be measured through an external wire/probe on the eSIM CLK and I/O lines rather than through a complete internal modem trace.
- The external waveform could show that unexpected bytes appeared on the I/O line, but by itself it could not establish:
  - whether the bytes were actually transmitted by the modem;
  - whether they were generated by the eSIM vendor/device;
  - whether they were caused by FIFO residue, IO drive/pull-up behavior, timing or another hardware/software condition;
  - whether the observed signal represented the cause of the mute or only a correlated signal / measurement artifact.
- The eSIM vendor attributed the temporary mute to extra bytes received from the modem. Because the available trace and measurement path did not identify the generating side or the causal chain, Alcedo could not immediately disprove the vendor's attribution with equivalent evidence. This created a self-verification / self-defense difficulty rather than proving the vendor's conclusion was correct.
- Alcedo therefore arranged a DOE-based investigation with the driver and hardware teams and planned joint work in the Zion lab. The DOE separated the relevant variables, including:
  - BWT fix;
  - Clock Stop Mode fix;
  - IO pull-up / drive strength;
  - FIFO and TX/RX debug traces;
  - additional register capture;
  - device and environment conditions.
- The investigation reproduced a consistent extra-byte pattern in the P26 debugging record: two extra bytes beginning with `0300`, observed approximately 40 μs after the TX block. Multiple sites participated; 6 of 8 monitored devices reproduced the behavior.
- The investigation also separated related but non-identical symptoms:
  - BWT calculation / timeout;
  - extra bytes;
  - garbage data / possible signal degradation;
  - Clock Stop Mode behavior;
  - temporary mute and factory-retest failure.
- The eSIM vendor analysis concluded that the extra bytes could cause the temporary mute. The Alcedo-side debug path subsequently provided stronger evidence for the signal behavior and the need to distinguish the observed waveform from the actual root cause.
- The debugging path included separate BWT and Clock Stop Mode patches, customer template-image integration, cross-site testing, circuit review and escalation to joint software/hardware analysis if software could not explain the waveform.

## Recommended lesson

> **For eSIM-only customer issues, waveform evidence alone is insufficient to establish causality. Without a structured end-to-end trace and a controlled measurement path, Alcedo may be unable to distinguish modem transmission, vendor/device behavior and measurement artifacts — making the root cause difficult to prove or refute.**

If the latest controlled Zion-lab result has formally confirmed an eSIM-vendor root cause, add it only after the corresponding final record is read back into the evidence ledger. The historical evidence currently supports the observability/root-cause attribution gap more strongly than an unconditional vendor-fault statement.

## Internal lesson / optimization

For P27/P28, establish an eSIM/SIM debug-readiness package before customer testing:

- structured transaction trace from AP request through Modem/eSIM response;
- synchronized timestamps across AP, Modem, driver and eSIM vendor logs;
- CLK/I/O waveform capture method and probe limitations;
- ability to identify the transmitting side of unexpected bytes;
- device / circuit / ATR / profile configuration;
- DOE template with controlled variables and expected observations;
- criteria to distinguish cause, correlation and measurement artifact;
- joint owner for software, driver, hardware and vendor convergence.

The evidence package should be sufficient for Alcedo to confirm or challenge a root-cause attribution with equivalent technical evidence.

## External lesson / optimization

Alcedo, Zion and the eSIM vendor should agree before the next high-risk test:

1. Which logs and waveform signals are required.
2. How AP, Modem, driver and eSIM-vendor timestamps are synchronized.
3. Which side owns each measurement point.
4. What constitutes proof that bytes were transmitted by the Modem.
5. How measurement noise or probe artifacts are excluded.
6. Who owns the next DOE and customer retest.
7. What evidence is required before assigning root cause or requesting a fix.

This changes the discussion from “which side is responsible?” to “what evidence can distinguish the possible causes?”

## P27/P28 implication

Add an **eSIM Joint Debugging and Observability Checklist** to the Joint Verification Readiness Gate. A high-risk eSIM issue should not enter formal root-cause assignment until the minimum trace and measurement conditions are available, or the confidence limitation is explicitly recorded.
